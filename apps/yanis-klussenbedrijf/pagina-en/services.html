Full renovation

The whole route: strip out, build up, install and finish. That is what this company is built for. You do not have to chase a tiler, an electrician and a painter one after the other and hope their diaries line up.

  • Stripping out and removing whatever has to go
  • A new layout with walls and openings
  • Electrics and pipework for the new situation
  • Tiling, floors and the whole finishing package
  • Handing over clean and walking through it together

Electrical work

A renovation changes the layout, and then the old electrics almost never fit any more. Sockets end up behind a new wall, the kitchen asks for circuits of its own, and the lighting has to follow the new plan.

  • Chasing in cabling and making it disappear
  • Moving or adding sockets, switches and lighting points
  • Extra circuits for a kitchen, bathroom or machinery
  • Connecting and fitting the lighting

Plumbing

Water and waste decide where a bathroom, toilet or kitchen can go. This is the work that later disappears under the tiles, so it gets the most attention before anything is closed up.

  • Laying or rerouting water and waste pipes
  • Connecting up the bathroom, toilet and kitchen
  • Setting and fitting the sanitaryware
  • Pressure testing and checking before the wall goes shut

Tiling

Tiling shows at a glance how carefully the work was done. We set the job out first, so the full tiles and the cuts land where you want to see them and not wherever they happen to come out.

  • Wall and floor tiles, small formats up to large slabs
  • Levelling the substrate, and waterproofing it where that is needed
  • Grouting, silicone and the finish at corners and trims
  • Can be hired on its own if the rest is already standing

Painting

The last coat decides how everything underneath looks. A crooked joint or a patch that was not prepared properly is exactly what the paint brings out.

  • Interior walls and ceilings
  • Woodwork, frames and doors
  • Preparation: sanding, filling and priming
  • Masking up and keeping the dust down in an occupied building
