What is the minimum order?
A half box for a trial and a full box for a standing programme. Mixed boxes are fine, so you can trial three lines in one shipment. Exact box counts per variety are on the catalogue page.
How long from order to delivery?
Order confirmed by Monday flies the same week. Nairobi to Amsterdam is a night flight, and your delivery day is confirmed on the order.
Do you ship direct or through the Dutch auction?
Direct. Your box is built for you and flown to you. Nothing goes through the clock, which saves a day of handling and a margin.
Which airports do you fly into?
Amsterdam Schiphol and Liege as standard, with onward trucking across Europe. Other airports on request if the volume supports it.
How is the cold chain protected?
Pre-cooled within an hour of cutting, sealed into pre-chilled boxes, held at 2 to 4 C airside and in the aircraft hold. Temperature is logged and the log travels with the shipment.
Can we get our own grading spec?
Yes. Stem length, bunch weight, stem count and cut stage are set per customer and written into the order, and that spec is what the packhouse checks against.

Altitude does<br>the work
Between Naivasha and the slopes of Mount Kenya the days are bright and the nights drop close to ten degrees. That swing slows the plant, which gives a tighter head, a thicker neck and a deeper colour.
It is why a Kenyan stem still looks fresh on day six in a Dutch shop, and why we buy above 1,800 metres even when a lower farm quotes less.

Shipping, packing and cold chain | JASM Flowers
How flowers get from a Kenyan field to a European cold store: cut to order, pre-cooled within the hour, night freighter from Nairobi to Amsterdam or Liege on an unbroken cold chain.
Kenya to Europe,<br>cold the whole way
Most vase life is lost before a flower leaves the country of origin. Here is what happens to your stems between the field and your cold store.
Seven steps, one unbroken<br>temperature line
Day 0, 06:00
Cut
Stems cut in the cool of the morning, straight into clean water with a hydration treatment.
Day 0, 09:00
Graded and bunched
Length and bunch weight checked against your written spec. Anything off spec is left behind.
Day 0, 10:00
Pre-cooled
Into the cold room at 2 to 4 C within an hour of cutting. Vase life is won here.
Day 0, 16:00
Packed
Sealed into pre-chilled boxes with your labels. Photo of the pallet on request before it moves.
Day 0, 23:00
Airside at JKIA
Handed to the airline in a temperature-controlled build-up area, on the night freighter.
Day 1, morning
Lands in Europe
Amsterdam or Liege. Cleared and moved into a cold store the same morning.
On to you
Delivered
Trucked to you refrigerated, with the temperature log from the whole journey. Your delivery day is confirmed on the order.

A short chain,<br>on purpose
Most Kenyan flowers reach a European florist through three or four hands: farm, exporter, auction, wholesaler. Every hand adds a day and a margin.
You order from us, our growing partners cut to that order, and the box is built for you. One fewer week in transit, and a price for the stem.
We are not a farm. We bring the relationship with the growers, the specification the crop is held to, and the export process that gets it to you in the condition you were promised.
Grading and bunching flowers in the packhouse
We work with selected Kenyan growers rather than owning farms, and collaborate closely with them on quality, specifications, harvesting, grading and export preparation, so what leaves the country matches what was agreed.
A small group of growers
We work with growers we know, each chosen for the crop they are genuinely good at.
Grown to a written specification
Stem length, bunch weight, stem count and cut stage are agreed up front and shared with the grower, so the spec is set before the crop is planted.
Close on quality and export prep
We stay involved through harvesting, grading and export preparation. If a farm will not take an unannounced visit, we do not buy from it.
Naivasha, around 1,900 m
Lakeside blocks on volcanic soil. The main solidago, limonium and statice ground, and the reason filler volume holds all year.
Mount Kenya, 2,100&ndash;2,400 m
Cooler and higher. Eucalyptus, delphinium, hydrangea and the roses that need real altitude to build a head worth selling.
Two regions, deliberately
Weather that ruins a crop in Naivasha rarely touches Mount Kenya in the same week, so one storm does not break a standing order.
Graded against<br>your spec
Your stem length, bunch weight, stem count and cut stage are written into the order, and that sheet is what the grading table checks against.
Anything off spec does not travel. It is cheaper to leave a bunch in Nairobi than to credit a box in Rotterdam.
